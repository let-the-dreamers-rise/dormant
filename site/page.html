Open instruction semantics for Solana · MIT

Solana can tell you which program ran. Nothing tells you what it did.

Every wallet, explorer, indexer and simulator on this chain needs to turn instruction bytes into a statement of effect. Today each of them rebuilds a partial, private answer, and 36.2% of mainnet instructions defeat all of them. Dormant is the open dataset of effect semantics for the programs that carry most of mainnet, plus a reference decoder that reads them. The decoder below is not a screenshot. It is the library, running in this page, with no server behind it.

Measured, not asserted

Most of what happens on Solana cannot be read by the tools that watch it.

I sampled live mainnet blocks and asked one question of every instruction: can a stock RPC node, with jsonParsed on, say what this does? Across two independent runs, 36.2% and 42.1% of instructions came back opaque — a program id and an undifferentiated blob of bytes. In the conservative run, 87.6% of transactions contained at least one.

Those numbers are deliberately unflattering to my own argument. Vote traffic is excluded, because it is 57% of a block and nobody signs it. ComputeBudget is excluded, because it was 43% of the apparent gap and is natively decoded. Leaving both in would have let me claim 56%. The denominator is the part of this that is easy to cheat, so it is the part I wrote down first.

The ladder on the right is what closing it is worth. Caching every published IDL is real progress and it is not enough, because 15 of the 25 busiest programs publish no IDL at all.

Prior art

Decoding Solana instructions is a solved problem. Saying what they do is not.

Several teams already turn bytes into names, and some do it very well. None of them publishes the effect of an instruction, or the condition that effect depends on. That is the whole of what I am proposing to build, and it is why this is not a reimplementation.

ProjectWhat it doesWhat it does not do
HeliusDecodes 3,600+ programs into named, typed events. Commercial, hosted.Names the call. Does not state what signing it transfers, or under what condition.

An IDL names; it never states effect

An IDL will tell you an instruction is called set_authority and takes three accounts. It does not say that account 2 gains permanent control, or that nothing requires account 2 to sign. That gap is not a tooling oversight — the information is simply not in the file.

15 of the top 25 publish no IDL

I derived the Anchor IDL account for each of the 25 busiest programs and checked the chain. Ten publish one; fifteen do not. Those fifteen are 12.4% of all mainnet instruction volume — invisible to every IDL-based parser above, and reachable only by reverse engineering.

The obvious objection

Anyone could copy this. That is the point of funding it.

There is no moat here and there is not supposed to be one. The output is MIT, the dumps are free, and the API has no key. If this work were defensible I should be raising venture money instead of asking a foundation for a grant.

The money does not buy code. It buys twenty weeks of reverse engineering. Ten of the twenty-five busiest programs publish an on-chain IDL and are comparatively cheap. The other fifteen publish nothing and have to be recovered from instruction data and observed state changes, one program at a time. That labour is copyable in principle and has not happened in practice — which is exactly why 12.4% of mainnet instruction volume is still unreadable.

It is also not a hypothetical want. The Foundation published its own Discriminator Database RFP at $60,000, so the need is already priced and still unmet. I am proposing to deliver against it for $45,000 with the measurement of the gap already done, published, and reproducible by you in ten minutes.

What accrues afterwards is not ownership. It is a maintained dataset and somebody who knows these twenty-five programs well enough to keep it correct as they ship new versions.

Who consumes a semantics dataset

  • WalletsSay what a transaction does before the signature, instead of showing a base58 blob and a confirm button.
  • ExplorersRender history as statements of effect rather than as program ids and hex.
  • IndexersEmit labelled economic events without hand-writing a parser per protocol.
  • Simulators and risk enginesReason about what a pending transaction would change, not merely which programs it touches.
  • Security reviewersThe narrow case this started as, and the smallest of the five.

Where the money goes. The per-program prices above are how the total was derived — tiered by a property I measured rather than estimated. The table beside this is what it is actually spent on, so both the derivation and the composition are on record.

Paid archival RPC is not optional for this work: recovering fifteen undocumented programs means pulling large volumes of historical transactions and replaying observed state changes, and public endpoints rate-limit and drop large blocks — a failure I hit while running the measurement that this proposal rests on.

The independent review is a real external cost and it is deliberate. A semantics dataset that only I have checked is worth considerably less than one somebody competent has tried to break, and the review is published in full including anything it finds unfixed.
